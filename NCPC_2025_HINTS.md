# NCPC 2025: second-round algorithm hints

These paragraphs are written for the harness's C++17 submissions and are copied into `config.json` under `hidden_hints`. The runner appends only the selected problem's hint to its round-two solving prompt. Round-one solving and problem selection remain unhinted. Choose a new `output_directory` when enabling these hints for an experiment that has already started, because hints are part of the frozen configuration. A nonempty per-problem `hidden_hint` overrides the corresponding top-level hint.

The algorithm descriptions were checked against the supplied public problem statements and reference solutions; the Dune Dash endpoint construction and Hidden Permutation divisor inversion are equivalent approaches derived from the problem structure. These are strong solution-outline hints, not guarantees that a model will produce an accepted program. No hidden test cases or reference source code are placed in the prompts. The [official contest page](https://nordic.icpc.io/ncpc2025/compete) links the statements, solution slides, and judges' solutions.

C++17 is the practical choice for this harness because its prompt, source extraction, packaging, and judge-language setup require C++. Python is convenient for the small arithmetic/formatting tasks and for arbitrary-precision integers, but using it here would require changes to those parts of the runner. The hints use C++17 throughout; where `__int128` is mentioned, use a GCC-compatible compiler supporting that extension.

## A. Arithmetic Adaptation (`arithmeticadaptation`)

Use bounded enumeration rather than a special-case-heavy formula: try each nonzero integer a from -999 through 999, set b = s - a, and output the first pair for which b is nonzero and lies in [-999, 999]. This needs only 1999 candidates and constant memory. Negative integers are allowed, and s = 0 still needs two nonzero numbers; the endpoints s = -999 and s = 999 must not cause an answer outside the permitted range. Output just the two integers, with no explanatory text. C++17 with ordinary int arithmetic is a simple, reliable choice.

## B. Bohemian Bookshelf (`bohemianbookshelf`)

Enumerate a book b that will be the tallest book in the stack, fixing its width L = h[b], and run subset-sum DP over total stacked thickness S from 0 to H. Every book must be assigned: stacking is allowed only when h <= L, standing only when h <= H, and b must be stacked; thus books too tall to stand are forced into the stack and books taller than L are forced upright. If T is the sum of all thicknesses, accept a reachable S with 0 < S < T and T - S + L <= W, then reconstruct the assignments and sort the stacked indices by decreasing height for bottom-to-top output. This costs O(N^2 H) across the candidates. Do not confuse stack width with stacked thickness, omit books, allow an empty group, or lose original indices during sorting. C++17 is well suited to the small DP and parent reconstruction.

## C. Crochet Competition (`crochetcompetition`)

Map Monday through Sunday to 0 through 6 and convert each timestamp to minutes from Monday midnight. Compute the forward difference modulo 10080, replacing zero by 10080 because identical timestamps mean one whole week. Decompose it greedily into days, then hours, then minutes, and build a list containing only nonzero components with the appropriate singular or plural suffix. One component prints alone, two are joined with ' and ', and three are joined with ', ' throughout: there is no extra 'and' in the three-component case. Week wraparound, leading zeros in clock input, and exactly 1 day/hour/minute are common mistakes; do not use real calendar dates or daylight-saving rules. C++17 with integer arithmetic and a small vector of strings is sufficient.

## D. Dune Dash (`dunedash`)

Use the promised triple-distance property to recover an endpoint: from any input checkpoint, a farthest checkpoint must be one of the two route endpoints, because distances from the chosen checkpoint strictly increase along either side of it. From that endpoint, sort all checkpoints by increasing squared distance; the same property makes this the route order, up to reversal. Sum Euclidean distances between consecutive points, giving O(N log N) time. Coordinate or polar-angle sorting and an O(N^2) distance search are unnecessary. Compare squared distances exactly, casting before subtraction/multiplication; signed 64-bit holds the stated squared-distance bound, while __int128 gives extra arithmetic safety. Take square roots only for the final length and print at least 15 significant digits. C++17 is a good choice for the large sort and precise geometry arithmetic.

## E. Egyptian Equality (`egyptianequality`)

Reject an odd total of C cells, then seek a connected prefix containing half the stones in several geometry-specific cell orders, always preserving '#' and validating both output regions by four-neighbour BFS before accepting. A reference-style O(N^2) construction tries five sweeps: columns left-to-right, visiting bottom-to-top for c < N and top-to-bottom otherwise; the mirrored column sweep right-to-left, visiting bottom-to-top for c >= N-1; rows top-to-bottom and cells left-to-right with the first two cells of every non-top row swapped; diagonals in increasing c-(N-1-r), with r increasing within each diagonal; and mirrored diagonals in increasing (N-1+r)-c, again with r increasing. Here r and c are zero-based; skip outside cells and stop each prefix immediately on its target-th C. Mark that prefix B and the rest A, trying the next sweep if either region is disconnected. Even parity alone is insufficient, and a generic snake ordering can disconnect the complement. C++17 with iterative BFS avoids recursion-depth problems.

## F. Follower Forensics (`followerforensics`)

Treat the input as a simple directed degree-sequence realization problem with weak connectivity, not strong connectivity or an undirected degree sequence. First require equal outgoing/incoming totals, all degrees at most n-1, and, for n > 1, no isolated vertex and at least n-1 edges. Use an Erdos-Miklos-Toroczkai-style directed greedy construction: select the smallest positive residual incoming or outgoing degree, breaking ties by the opposite residual degree and vertex ID; fulfil it using distinct other vertices with largest opposite residual degree, breaking ties by their other residual degree and ID. Remove the selected vertex and hold all chosen partners out of the ordered sets until its whole batch is complete, then decrement residual degrees and reinsert the updated vertices. Maintain both ascending selection orders and descending partner orders in O((n+m) log n), and track weak components with DSU. Reject exhausted partners, nonzero final residuals, or a disconnected result; n = 1 with zero edges is valid. Self-loops, duplicate directed edges, stale ordered-set keys, and a plain maximum-degree greedy that neglects connectivity are pitfalls. C++17 is the practical choice for ordered sets and up to a million output edges.

## G. Gotta Trade Some of 'Em (`gottatradesomeofem`)

Trading can carry each variant's monsters along arbitrarily long friendship paths, so find undirected connected components with DSU or iterative DFS/BFS. An assignment exists exactly when every component contains at least k children; within each component, give its children variants 1, 2, ..., k cyclically, ensuring every variant occurs there. Component membership, not direct friendship with every variant holder, determines reachability. Include isolated children in the component check, handle k = 1, and avoid recursive DFS on a chain of 100000 vertices. The algorithm takes O(n+m) with traversal or near-linear time with DSU and needs no flow, matching, or graph colouring. C++17 with DSU and grouped vertex IDs is a safe choice.

## H. Hidden Permutation (`hiddenpermutation`)

Recover cycle counts from fixed-string counts: for q = 1..N compute F(q) as the sum of supplied counts whose periods divide q, require it to be a positive exact power of two, and let L(q) be its integer base-2 logarithm. If c[l] counts length-l cycles, L(q) = sum_l c[l]*gcd(l,q). Using gcd(l,q) = sum_{d|l,d|q} phi(d), obtain S[d] = sum_{d|l} c[l] in increasing d from S[d] = (L(d) - sum_{e|d,e<d} phi(e)*S[e])/phi(d), requiring exact divisibility; then recover c[d] in decreasing d by subtracting counts at proper multiples. Require nonnegative integer cycle counts and sum_d d*c[d] = N, and construct disjoint cycles of those lengths. Finally enumerate divisors of the cycle-length LCM and recompute exact-period counts from 2^(sum_l c[l]*gcd(l,p)), subtracting smaller divisor counts, to check all supplied periods/counts and reject missing periods. Checking only q <= N misses corrupt counts at larger periods. Use C++17 with __int128 for exact counts and shifts: 2^100 and the input-count sums fit, but 64-bit integers and floating-point logarithms do not.

## I. Instagraph (`instagraph`)

The centrality of v is exactly the number of incoming edges u -> v whose reverse v -> u is absent: all such followers can simultaneously be in the celebrity's group, and mutual followers cannot. Store all directed edges using a packed 64-bit key u*N+v in a hash set, or sort these keys and binary-search reversed keys, then increment v's score only for nonreciprocated incoming edges. Expected O(N+M) hashing or O(M log M) sorting avoids an infeasible N-by-N matrix. Cast to 64-bit before forming keys, preserve the input's edge direction, and scan vertex IDs in increasing order, replacing the answer only on a strictly larger score so ties choose the smallest ID. With no edges the answer is vertex 1 with score 0. C++17 offers efficient memory use for up to a million edges.

## J. Jump (`jump`)

Jump between beat landings instead of simulating every jump: immediately after a beat the time residue is zero, and the next beat after s jumps satisfies s*E - s*(s-1)/2 congruent to 0 modulo N. For each energy residue E modulo N, precompute the smallest positive s <= 2N by enumerating s and solving 2*s*E congruent to s*(s-1) modulo 2N with gcd reduction and a modular inverse, populating every solution residue. If E <= s-1, he collapses before that beat and the remaining time is E*(E+1)/2; otherwise add s*E - s*(s-1)/2 and update E to E-s+2. The next energy residue is deterministic, so detect a repeated residue, record the cycle's energy change, its affine time cost, and the lowest energy reached inside it. A nonnegative cycle energy change implies infinity once the full cycle is feasible; a negative change allows skipping many cycles with an arithmetic-series time sum, stopping before any jump could launch at zero energy. Raw simulation with E up to 10^9 and cycle detection on residue alone without tracking energy are incorrect approaches. C++17 with 64-bit state and __int128 intermediate sums is recommended.

## K. km/h (`kmh`)

Maintain the greatest numeric speed-limit sign seen so far. A numeric sign directly sets the current legal speed to its own number; a '/' sign restores the unknown national speed limit, whose smallest possible value is the smallest multiple of ten strictly greater than that historical maximum, namely 10*(maximum/10+1). That lower bound is the fastest speed guaranteed legal from the information seen so far. Process signs online and print one answer per sign: later evidence must not change earlier answers, and a lower recent sign must not erase an older higher maximum. In particular, if the maximum is already a multiple of ten, the national lower bound is still the next multiple, not the same one. C++17 with ordinary integers is sufficient.
